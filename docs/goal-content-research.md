# Bengali Practice: goal-by-goal content review and research

Prepared 23 October 2026. This is a critical review, not a sales page. It says where the app is strong, where it is thin, and what would make the biggest difference to a buyer.

## How to read this, and how far to trust it

Every claim below carries one of three labels.

- **[Code]** I counted or read it in this repository. This is reliable.
- **[Web]** I found it through web searches in this session. Most of it comes from review blogs and vendor pages, which have a commercial interest. Where numbers are unverified I say so.
- **[Judgment]** My own opinion, from reading the app and the research. Treat it as a recommendation to challenge, not a fact.

Limits you should know about:

- I could not open Preply, Duolingo, Ling or the review sites page by page. The page reader failed to resolve those hosts, so I worked from search-result summaries. I did not fill the gaps from memory. Where a search could not confirm something, the text says so.
- I found **no independent, peer-reviewed study** of Bengali app learners, or of how heritage Bengali learners acquire reading. The "what learners want" sections are therefore reasoning from general language-learning evidence plus the competitor landscape.
- Prices for other products change often. Check them again before you quote them to anyone.

## 2. What the market looks like [Web]

### Preply (live tutoring)
- One-to-one live lessons with a tutor in Preply's own video classroom. Bengali tutors are listed at roughly $3 to $40 or more per lesson; one search result showed about $20 for a 50-minute business Bengali lesson. Check current prices.
- Preply sells Bengali by need, with separate pages for **beginners, conversation, business Bengali, intensive and intermediate**. That is the same idea as your goals, and it shows buyers expect to be asked "why are you learning?"
- Its AI tools support the tutor rather than replace them: **Lesson Insights** (an AI summary after each lesson with grammar corrections, vocabulary highlights and next steps), **Daily Exercises** (short practice between lessons) and **Scenario Practice** (tutor-assigned speaking practice, in beta). These launched for English learners first; I could not confirm they exist for Bengali.
- What this means for you: Preply's strength is a human who corrects you. Its weak point for a self-study learner is what happens **between** lessons. Your app is exactly that: the practice between lessons.

### Ling (the main Bengali app)
- Roughly 50 units and about 200 lessons, aligned to A1 to B1, per its own blog and one review. [Unverified count]
- Strong on listening, vocabulary and speaking prompts, with some voice recognition. Weak on the script: reviewers say finger tracing is basic.
- A chatbot exists, but sources disagree on whether it is open-ended or multiple choice.
- Price per Ling's own blog: about $16.99 a month, $89.99 a year. [Vendor figure]

### Mondly, Bluebird, Drops, Glossika, 50Languages
- Mondly: short lessons, everyday vocabulary, sold as a subscription or an occasionally discounted lifetime deal. Bluebird: a larger free tier with paid upgrades, with some doubt over its pronunciation guide. Glossika: repetition-based pronunciation. 50Languages: free vocabulary. [Web, review blogs only]
- The common complaint across these is **thin script teaching and romanization-only learning**. This is where your Writing course and step pictures are genuinely ahead.

### Children's and heritage apps
- Barnoparichay and Bornomala teach the alphabet and digits to young children. BALA Bornomala targets children growing up outside Bangladesh and West Bengal. A heritage-focused workbook exists for tracing. [Web]
- I found nothing aimed at **adult** heritage learners who can speak but cannot read. That gap is real, and it is the one your landing page already describes.

### Duolingo
- I found **no evidence of a Duolingo course that teaches Bengali to English speakers**. Third-party blogs say it does not exist, and a 2018 article said Bengali was planned. I could not confirm the official course list, so check Duolingo's own catalog before claiming this publicly. [Web, unverified]
- Duolingo does offer an English course for Bangla speakers (launched 2024) and teaches other languages through Bengali. [Web]
- The lesson from Duolingo is its habit design, not its content: streaks, leagues, daily quests, streak freezes, and friend streaks. The widely repeated figures (for example "streaks make users 3x more likely to return" or churn falling from 47% to 28%) come from marketing blogs without sources, so **do not quote them**. The direction is plausible. The size is unknown.
- Research on gamification in language learning suggests it raises **engagement** more than it raises **retention of knowledge**. Spaced repetition has the stronger evidence for retention, but only for people who keep using it. [Web: a 2024 Frontiers review and a systematic review; both are summarised, not fully read]

### Other options your buyers can find
- The Bhaktivedanta Research Centre launched an online Bengali course with Scottish Church College in November 2025, aimed at reading the Gaudiya Vaishnava texts. It is self-paced and the first of three planned levels. I found **no course for kirtan pronunciation or bhajan singing**. [Web]
- Udemy has short "survival Bengali" and script courses. The FSI Bengali course is free. Babel Group sells "Doing Business in Bangladesh" training that covers etiquette, not the language. [Web]
- Dialect: sources agree Bangladeshi and West Bengal Bengali are mutually intelligible, with vocabulary the most visible difference (for example জল or পানি for water, with Arabic and Persian loanwords more common in Bangladesh). Advice is to pick the standard that matches the learner's goal. [Web, blogs]
- Conjuncts are widely described as a major hurdle, and one source advises learning them gradually inside real words. [Web]

## 3. Goal by goal

Each goal gets a score out of 5 for **content depth today** and for **how convincing it is to a buyer**. These are my judgment. The gap list is ordered by how much it would change a buyer's decision.

### Goal 1. Talk with my family (Family)
**Today [Code]:** 24 lessons. Greetings, family, feelings, questions, 52 verbs, persons and the aap/tumi/tui choice, present tense, food, house, time, festivals, 4 family role plays (call parents, grandparents, in-laws, plan a visit), say-it-aloud practice.

**Depth 4/5. Buyer appeal 4/5.** This is the best-developed goal and the one most buyers will recognize themselves in.

**Gaps (most useful first):**
1. **Dialect choice.** A student whose family is from Dhaka and a student whose family is from Kolkata will use different words and hear different accents. A setting for "my family speaks Kolkata Bengali / Dhaka Bengali" that swaps a short list of common words and the audio voice would feel personal. [Judgment, based on the dialect sources]
2. **Real voices.** All audio is synthetic or generated. For family goals, a handful of recordings of **you** saying the greetings and calls, or of an elder, would build trust more than any feature. Low cost, needs your time.
3. **Reading family messages.** The landing page promises "reading your family's messages". There is no practice on WhatsApp-style messages, romanized Bengali typing (which many families use) or festival greeting cards. A "message decoder" set of 20 realistic short messages is cheap to build.
4. **Relationship words by side.** Maternal and paternal relatives have different words, and this confuses learners. The family vocabulary has 41 items; a clear tree diagram would help.

**Cheap now:** message decoder, a printable "first call" phrase card, a family tree diagram.
**Needs your time:** recordings of real voices. **Heavy:** an AI partner who plays "your grandmother" (see section 5).

### Goal 2. Devotional communication (Devotional)
**Today [Code]:** 27 lessons, the largest path. Devotional greetings and address, ashram, temple and prasad role plays, kirtan vocabulary and phrases, names, festivals, reading ladders and decoders, bhajan reading, guru and path vocabulary, polite verb forms.

**Depth 4/5. Buyer appeal 4/5, and the most distinctive.** The one comparable offer I found (the BRC course) is about reading texts, and I found nothing for kirtan. You may be the only product covering the spoken devotional register.

**Gaps:**
1. **Sing-along reading.** Line-by-line bhajan text with the audio highlighted word by word, a slow mode, and a transliteration toggle. This is the "bhajans and mantras in the original" promise made concrete. [Judgment]
2. **Rights and review.** The texts must be public domain or cleared, and the teacher must review the Bengali. Say this clearly on the page; it is a trust point for this audience.
3. **Pronunciation of classical forms.** Devotional Bengali often uses forms (such as Sanskrit-derived words) that differ from everyday speech. A short "how this differs from daily speech" lesson would prevent confusion.
4. **Listening to kirtan.** Short, licensed or self-recorded audio of kirtan lines to follow.

**Cheap now:** word-by-word highlighted reading using the existing audio, a "daily verse" email. **Needs your time:** recording kirtan lines. **Heavy:** anything that scores singing.

### Goal 3. Professional communication (Work)
**Today [Code]:** 18 lessons. Script, greetings, formality (aapni), questions, people and jobs, work and technology words, numbers and time, money, 52 verbs, polite commands, future tense, negatives, sentences, one phone role play, impromptu speaking. The path itself admits it is shorter than it should be.

**Depth 2/5. Buyer appeal 2/5 today.** This is the weakest goal compared with how it is described on the landing page. A professional will compare it to a Preply business Bengali tutor, and will notice that there is **one** work role play.

**Gaps:**
1. **Workplace role plays.** At minimum: introducing yourself at a first meeting, a client phone call, scheduling a meeting, agreeing and disagreeing politely, asking someone to repeat or slow down, small talk before business, thanking and following up. Seven to eight is a credible set. [Judgment]
2. **Writing at work.** Short WhatsApp and email templates in Bengali (greeting, request, confirmation, apology), with the romanized version. Most real business contact is typed messages.
3. **Etiquette notes.** Greetings, names and titles, same-sex greeting customs, business cards and gifts. Free guides (for example Commisceo and Babel) cover this for Bangladesh. You can write your own for **Kolkata and West Bengal** business culture, which those guides do not cover.
4. **Industry packs.** One short vocabulary set per field, such as medical, legal, IT, manufacturing, would let you say "for people who work in X". Start with the one or two fields your own students use.
5. **Be honest on the page.** Until the above exists, "professional" should be described as "polite, clear everyday Bengali for work", not business fluency.

**Cheap now:** etiquette lesson, message templates, role plays written from real scenarios. **Needs your time:** reviewing Bengali for each role play. **Pair with live teaching:** this is where a Preply session adds the most.

### Goal 4. Learn it for the love of it (Curiosity)
**Today [Code]:** 16 lessons. Script, letter sounds, greetings, numbers, food and festival words, three grammar topics, three fables, flashcards, one impromptu session.

**Depth 3/5 for a sampler, but thin if someone stays.** This learner has no deadline, so the product has to be enjoyable on its own. A curious learner is the easiest to lose.

**Gaps:**
1. **Culture, not just language.** Short notes on poetry (Tagore and Nazrul), proverbs, songs, sweets, festivals, the Bengali calendar, and why the script looks the way it does. This is the reason a curious learner picked Bengali. Cheap and good for search traffic too.
2. **Loanwords and surprises.** "Bengali words you already know" (for example words English took from Bengali or Hindustani) gives quick wins.
3. **A reason to come back.** A "word of the day" and a small weekly challenge (read this verse, say these five words) that do not need the learner to have a goal.
4. **Light progression.** A "Bengali in 30 days" tour that is shorter than the full path and ends with a small certificate or shareable card.

**Cheap now:** all four. **Heavy:** none needed.

### Goal 5. Travel and the market (Travel)
**Today [Code]:** 15 lessons. Numbers and money, places, directions, transport, food, body (for the doctor), and 7 role plays (rickshaw, directions, train, shop, restaurant, hotel, doctor).

**Depth 3/5. Buyer appeal 3/5.** The role plays are the right ones. What is missing is the **quick-reference use**: a traveller wants to find a phrase in two taps, not take a course.

**Gaps:**
1. **A phrasebook mode.** Searchable, grouped phrase cards with big Bengali text and audio, usable with one hand. The word finder exists for vocabulary. A travel-specific version with "show this to someone" full-screen cards would be the most useful travel feature. [Judgment]
2. **Offline use.** Travellers often have no signal. Making the phrase cards work offline is a real selling point, and it needs a service worker and cached audio, which is moderate work.
3. **Bangladesh or India.** Currency (taka or rupee), vocabulary and fares differ. The numbers and money lessons should show both. [Web: dialect and currency sources]
4. **Signs and menus.** Reading practice on station boards, shop signs, street names and menu items.
5. **Emergency and health.** Lost passport, police, pharmacy, "I am allergic to…". There is a doctor role play, but no emergency card.
6. **Bargaining numbers drill.** A fast listening game for prices in the market, and negotiating phrases.

**Cheap now:** phrase cards, a sign-reading set, an emergency card. **Moderate:** offline mode.

### Goal 6. Speak Bengali (Speaking)
**Today [Code]:** 14 lessons. Sounds, numbers, first words, four role plays, flashcards, impromptu, story relay, exam.

**Depth 2/5. Buyer appeal 2/5.** This is the biggest honest weakness. The goal is **"speak"**, yet the app **never listens**. The learner hears a model, reads a script, and decides for themselves whether they said it right. Reviewers of other apps cite exactly this as the gap, and Ling's selling point is voice recognition. [Web]

**Gaps, in cost order:**
1. **Record and compare (cheap, no cloud).** Use the phone's microphone to record the learner, then play it beside the model clip. It stays on the device and costs nothing to run. The learner does the judging, but hearing the difference is itself instructive, and it makes the app feel like it listens. [Judgment]
2. **Shadowing mode.** Play a short line, a pause, the learner repeats. Pace and repeat settings. Cheap.
3. **Browser speech recognition (cheap, uncertain).** The Web Speech API can recognise speech in Chrome, Edge and Safari. I could **not confirm that Bengali (bn-IN or bn-BD) works well**, and on Chrome audio goes to Google's servers. Test it before promising anything. [Web: general support only]
4. **Scored pronunciation (heavy).** A cloud speech model that rates each attempt. This brings per-use cost, privacy questions and real engineering. See section 5.
5. **Answer-the-question drills.** Listen to a question, pick or say a reply, a step before full conversation.

### Goal 7. Read Bengali (Reading)
**Today [Code]:** 11 lessons. Script, vowel signs, numbers, 7 fables, flashcards.

**Depth 3/5 for beginners, 2/5 beyond. Buyer appeal 3/5.** The path ends after seven short stories, and I would expect a learner who finishes them to ask "now what?"

**Gaps:**
1. **Conjunct letters (যুক্তাক্ষর).** The single largest missing lesson for this goal. Sources describe conjuncts as a key difficulty and suggest learning them in context inside real words. A staged lesson (the 20 most common conjuncts, each with three words and audio) would unlock real reading. [Web + Code]
2. **More graded reading.** Seven fables is a sample. Reading is the goal, so aim for 20 to 30 short texts at three levels, including everyday items: signs, short news headlines, messages, recipes, children's rhymes.
3. **Tap-a-word.** Tap any word in a story to see the meaning and hear it. Great value, moderate effort because each word needs a gloss.
4. **Read-along with audio highlight.** Listen while the words light up. The audio exists.
5. **Handwriting and print fonts.** Real handwritten letters differ from the typeset ones the app shows. A short comparison lesson helps learners read notes and signs.
6. **A "can read" check.** A short passage the learner reads before and after, and a self-score.

### Goal 8. Speak and write correctly (Grammar)
**Today [Code]:** 18 lessons on this path and 38 in the grammar course. Persons, verbs, tenses, karak, bibhakti, number, gender, negatives, word order, translation drills, tense workout, exam.

**Depth 4/5. Buyer appeal 3/5.** Strong, but grammar is also the part that scares casual learners. Presentation matters more than coverage.

**Gaps:**
1. **Formal and everyday Bengali (সাধু and চলিত).** Learners meet both in print. A short, clear lesson on "what you will see in books and what people say" is missing. I could not find a source on this in my searches, so verify against your own teaching. [Judgment]
2. **Error help without AI.** Add a "common mistakes" panel per topic, written from what your own students get wrong. This is the next best thing to personal feedback and costs nothing to run.
3. **Short daily grammar bites.** A single rule with three examples and one question, one a day.
4. **A link from grammar to speaking.** After each rule, a two-minute role play that **uses** it.

## 4. Cross-cutting: what would make buyers more confident

These apply to every goal and probably matter more than any single lesson.

1. **Show the end point.** A "what you can do" list per goal, in plain sentences ("order a meal, ask the price, understand the answer"), with a check-off as lessons complete. This is how language levels are normally explained, and it turns a long list of lessons into a visible finish line. [Judgment]
2. **A short placement quiz.** Five minutes at the start that sets the first stage of My path and the "can you read yet?" answer automatically. It also feels personal.
3. **Proof of progress.** A "before and after" recording, a certificate of completion for each path, a shareable progress card. Cheap, and free marketing.
4. **A human in the loop.** The thing Preply offers and apps do not is a person. Offer **one** low-cost option inside the app: a monthly "ask the teacher" question, a weekly office hour, or a clear path to book a live lesson from the lesson page. This also supports your Preply listing.
5. **Habit mechanics that are cheap.** Streak and leaderboard exist. Add a daily quest ("do three small things"), a weekly summary email (what you learned, what is next), and a "welcome back" nudge after a break. The reminder email system is already in place.
6. **Trust.** Real student quotes (the landing page has some), a refund promise (you already have 7 days), the teacher's face and voice, and honest wording about what is still being built.
7. **Dialect clarity.** Say on the landing page which Bengali you teach, and offer the switch where it matters.

## 5. Features that need heavy setup (costly, so decide with care)

These are the "AI and cloud" features. I list them so you can say no with your eyes open. None of these figures are quotes; they are cost drivers, and you would need current vendor pricing.

| Feature | What it gives the buyer | What it costs you | My view |
|---|---|---|---|
| AI conversation partner (text) | Open practice at any hour, in role | A paid language-model API on every message, a safety and quality review of Bengali output, abuse limits | Valuable but risky: Bengali quality varies and wrong corrections harm trust. Not now. |
| AI conversation partner (voice) | Speaking practice with real listening | Speech recognition plus synthesis plus a language model per turn; the highest running cost of anything here | Not now |
| Pronunciation scoring | Tells the learner which sounds are wrong | A cloud speech service, calibration against Bengali accents, scores that may be unreliable | Not now. Use record-and-compare instead. |
| Automatic feedback on writing or handwriting | Checks shapes and spelling | Handwriting recognition for a script with many shapes; a model that can tell a poor stroke from a good one | Not now. The stroke pictures and tracing already do most of the teaching. |
| Personalised AI lesson summaries (as Preply does) | "What you got wrong today and what to do next" | Needs recorded lessons, transcription and a model | Only if you record your own live lessons; otherwise skip. |
| Adaptive path (changes itself by performance) | Right lesson at the right time | Data and tuning work | Partly achievable with rules you write yourself, with no AI |
| Offline mode | Phrases work without signal | Service worker, cached audio, update logic | Worth doing for travel only |

**The pattern:** the expensive features are the ones that **listen to or judge** the learner. The inexpensive features are the ones that **give the learner better material**. I would invest in material first.

## 7. Questions for you

1. **Which Bengali do your buyers mostly need,** Kolkata, Dhaka or both? This decides whether the dialect setting is worth building.
2. **How much of your own recorded voice are you willing to add?** A few dozen real clips would be more convincing than any feature.
3. **Is "Professional" going to be a main selling goal?** If yes, it needs the work in section 3 before the landing page leans on it.
4. **Which goal do most of your current students actually pick?** The admin page shows the counts. Build for that first.
